## Finding 1: the working platform carries NO lock carriers

PSP directory diff (4700S 18 entries @ `0x8E0000`, BC250 19 entries):

- only in BC250: `0x20` (1616 B), **`0x24` SEC_GASKET (11856 B)**, **`0x45`
  TOS_SECURITY_POLICY (1760 B)**;
- only in 4700S: `0x4` (0x20000 B), `0xC` (0x200C0 B);
- common: `0x1 0x2 0x8 0x9 0x12 0x13 0x28 0x30–0x34 0x44 0x4F 0x50 0x51`.

The 4700S BIOS ships no SEC_GASKET and no TOS_SECURITY_POLICY at all. There is nothing
to unlock at the PSP-policy level on the 4700S: VCN is enabled by the normal boot chain
(ABL/SMU DPM/driver), and the two carriers are BC250-specific additions. This is the
BIOS-side shape of the operator's thesis: the lock is baked at boot, not held by the OS.

## Finding 2: the platform-generic blobs are byte-identical

- type `0x44` (26128 B): sha `4d8b8d271acc4bd9…` on BOTH images — identical bytes (the
  signed-table + SMU-workload-code container of lane V2b is generic AMD content).
- type `0x4F` (66048 B KDB): `4435c945ce914b29…` on BOTH — identical.
- type `0x9` root blob (1088 B) and type `0x13` (8512 B): same sizes on both.

So V2b's unsigned tail and the KDB region are not BC250-customized; the lock lives in
the two BC250-only carriers (and what feeds them), not in the generic blobs.

## Finding 3: our decoded SMU body IS the BC250 entry, verbatim

- BC250 type-`0x8` `$PS1` container (`0x8FEE00`, 0x40100 B = 0x100 header + 0x40080
  body): body sha == `cae8617c…` == our `smu_fw_robin_5` — byte-identical. The decode
  in `2026-09-23-smu-chain-development.md` is THE firmware the board runs.
- 4700S type-`0x8`: same container size (0x40100), different build (`6774ac44…`); both
  bodies start with a build-stamp pattern (`01075800 01075800…` vs `00124600
00124600…`).
- The collective's decoded message tables (`scratch/amd_smu_reverse_engineering/
tables/known_messages_smu_fw_robin_5.txt`) contain NO Vcn/PowerUp-named message —
  independently matching thelamer's decompile claim and our C3 closure. Neither SMU
  body carries VCN ASCII markers (tables, not strings — string scans are not evidence
  here).

## Finding 4: the "donor" unlock values are Cezanne's, and BC250 already carries one

From the project's own gasket-groups map (`import/…/docs/02-gasket-groups.md`):

- stock BC250 gasket: mailbox header `0x03E810 -> 0`, TWELVE `0x03E814` commands;
- donor (CEZANNE `0x24 @0x25A000`, NOT the 4700S): header -> 6 ("включение сегментов
  VCN"), ONE command `0x2544C382`, plus the 0x201 pairs; the xx234 family
  (`0x090xxxxx+0x234` = VCN instance enables): stock 47 addresses, donor 59, only-donor
  includes `0x0900C234`.
- byte scan: `0x2544C382` occurs 2x in the BC250 5.00 CLV image itself — `0x98491C`
  (inside the stock `0x24` gasket) and `0x9CACA4` (inside the stock `0x45` policy) —
  and 0x in the 4700S image.

So the spoof in the glitch-signed build re-points the BC250's own lock carriers at a
Cezanne-derived unlock policy (header 6 + the single VCN mailbox command). The 4700S
has no such machinery anywhere — its VCN needs no policy pass.

## Answer to the operator's question

On the 4700S "with the VCN block" nothing special happens — and that is the point: no
PSP entry locks it, no policy group gates it, the generic blobs are identical to ours;
the block comes up through the normal boot/driver chain. Every VCN-specific extra in
the BC250 BIOS is one of the two lock carriers and their policy payload. The BIOS-side
difference between "VCN works" and "VCN locked" is exactly the surface the glitch-signed
candidate already replaces.

## What this does NOT answer

- The "second gate" (clock/reset/isolation after power domain 6) is NOT in PSP policy;
  it is common-chain SMU/DPM territory — the 4700S BIOS does not contain a
  copy-pasteable register list for it (its SMU does it internally at boot).
- A full Ghidra diff of the two SMU builds (6774ac44 vs cae8617c) could localize VCN
  DPM code in the sibling build — follow-up if the V5/T1 runtime port stalls.
