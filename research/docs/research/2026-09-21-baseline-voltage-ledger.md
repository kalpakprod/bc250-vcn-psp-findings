Date: 2026-09-21 (Asia/Almaty)

Evidence labels follow `memory/evidence-model.md`: `hardware-verified`, `observed-bytes`, `static-analysis`, and `historical-report`.

## Conclusion

No retained record establishes an instrumented, revision-matched measurement of the physical SoC rail at exactly **1.15 V**. The corpus does contain a board-runtime SMU table read with two voltage telemetry words (`1275` and `912`, reported by the producer in mV), but the firmware source writer, channel-to-physical-rail mapping, board revision, and independent physical readback are unresolved. It must not be relabeled as a measured SoC voltage or exact rail voltage.

The 2026-09-05 chat evidence is a participant report of a **manual VRM feedback adjustment** and a self-reported SoC value of “1.2-something”; it is not an instrument log. The participant explicitly said BIOS voltage configuration was abandoned before the feedback adjustment. BIOS CBS offset fields and fixed-voltage fields are separate static firmware controls; their existence, stored bytes, or staged mailbox values do not establish that the physical SoC rail changed.

## Decisions and next evidence

1. Keep SoC `1.15 V` as an operator target/constraint only. Do not translate it into CBS offset bytes, VCORE/GFX/MEMIO fields, or a physical feedback change.
2. Classify the 2026-09-05 feedback story as `historical-report`, not `hardware-verified` or `observed-bytes`.
3. Classify `1275/912` as board-runtime firmware telemetry with unresolved rail identity, not an exact SoC/rail measurement.
4. The smallest decisive physical record would identify the board revision and PUS1/PUA1 markings, then record an authorized VDDCR_SOC probe/readback with date, instrument/model, probe reference, operating state, and raw readings at preserved baseline. A separate independent clock-domain measurement is still required for 1:1:1.
